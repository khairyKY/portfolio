Welcome 👋

This interactive guide covers LRA201 Introduction to Economics and Sustainable Development, Lectures 1–4: scarcity and opportunity cost, economic systems, the production possibilities frontier, GDP and growth, development and the HDI, and sustainable development and the SDGs. It follows the lecture slides (the PPF slides use Parkin's Microeconomics), the professor's PPF problem sheet, and two past quizzes. Where the course material has a mistake, the guide says so and gives the correct version. Navigate through the modes below:

  • 📖 Tutorial Mode: each step goes idea → worked example → why → quick check, with tap-to-trace opportunity-cost tables, a PPF explorer and an HDI calculator.
  • 🎴 Flash Cards: definitions, formulas and the worked answers you should know cold.
  • 🎯 Professor's Focus: Part 1 (Lectures 1–2 + the professor's PPF sheet) and Part 2 (Lectures 3–4 + the past quizzes), every problem worked in full.
  • ⚡ Practice Quiz: exam-style MCQs for every topic, plus all 43 MCQs from the two past quizzes in their original order.

💡 Short on time? Do Tutorials 3 and 4: every numerical question so far is a PPF question. Press Ctrl+K to search, and open the 📋 Cheat Sheet (bottom-right) for every definition and formula.

📋 Exam Reference Sheet

Core ideas (Lecture 1)

  • Economics: using scarce resources to produce goods and services and distribute them.
  • Scarcity → choice (tradeoff) → opportunity cost = the best alternative given up.
  • Incentive: a reward or penalty that shapes choices.
  • Factors → returns: land → rent · labour → wages · capital → interest · entrepreneurship → profit (+ knowledge).
  • Financial capital (money, loans) buys physical capital (machines). Human capital = skills.
  • Economic resource: price > 0. Free: price = 0 (air).
  • What (which goods) · How (technique) · For whom (incomes).
  • Needs: survival. Wants: can be given up.

Systems (Lecture 2)

  • Micro: one unit (one price, one firm). Macro: aggregates (GDP, inflation, unemployment rate).
  • Command: government decides (Cuba, N. Korea, China). Market: individuals, prices (USA, Canada). Mixed: both (Egypt, Saudi Arabia).
  • Command problems: no prices, information, cost, incentives, shortages/surpluses, ignores consumers.

PPF

  • Max combinations of two goods, all resources used efficiently; fixed technology and resources.
  • On: attainable, efficient · inside: attainable, inefficient · outside: unattainable.
  • OC of 1 X = Y given up ÷ X gained (positive: |slope|).
  • OC of X = 1 ÷ OC of Y between the same two points.
  • Constant OC → straight line. Increasing OC → bowed outward (resources specialised).
  • Better technology / more resources → shift out (growth). Lost resources → shift in.
  • Helps one good only → only that good's intercept moves.
  • Change of mix = movement along, not a shift.

GDP

  • Market value of final goods and services produced within a country in a period.
  • Intermediate goods excluded (double counting). Value added = sales − inputs bought.
  • GDP = C + I + G + (X − M) (expenditure) · wages + rent + interest + profit (income).
  • Nominal: current prices. Real: constant prices = nominal ÷ (deflator/100).
  • GDP per capita = GDP ÷ population

Growth

  • Growth = (GDP₂ − GDP₁) ÷ GDP₁ × 100% (the slide omits × 100).
  • Causes: capital, technology, education.
  • Inclusive: jobs for the unemployed (labour force = employed + unemployed); taxes/subsidies not sustainable.

Development

  • Higher living standards, self-esteem, choice: real income ↑, better distribution, quality of life, structural change.
  • Growth = quantity; development = quality, distribution (poor), structure, source of income.

HDI

  • Dimension index (actual − min) ÷ (max − min): life expectancy 20–85; school 0–18 (expected), 0–15 (mean); GNI 100–75,000 in ln.
  • UNDP: ∛(H × E × I). Slide: ⅓H + ⅓E + ⅓I (pre-2010).
  • Low < 0.550 · medium 0.550–0.699 · high 0.700–0.799 · very high ≥ 0.800.
  • Egypt 2021: 0.731 (high).

Traditional → sustainable

  • Traditional: growth + primary → secondary/tertiary + social gains; results: inequality, poverty, pollution, warming.
  • SD: meet present needs without compromising future generations (Brundtland 1987).
  • Inter-generational = between generations; intra-generational = within one.

Pillars

  • Social (equity, services, participation) · environmental (use ≤ regeneration, substitutes, pollution ≤ absorption) · economic (continuous, efficient, profitable).
  • Env + econ = viable · social + env = bearable · social + econ = equitable.
  • SDGs: 17 goals, 2015 → 2030, all countries. MDGs: 8 goals, to 2015, developing countries.
  • SDI = human development ÷ ecological overshoot. SDG Index 2023: Egypt 81/166 (69.6), Japan 21 (79.4).
